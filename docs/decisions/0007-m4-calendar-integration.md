# ADR 0007 — M4 calendar integration: scope, security boundaries and decisions

Status: **Accepted**, 2026-10-05 (owner approved the M4 readiness plan with decisions 1–6 as recorded here). Contract: `docs/m4/M4-BUILD-CONTRACT.md`.

## Context

M3's code is complete (Packages 0–10, PRs #30–#40); its owner acceptance is open, waiting on the restore rehearsal (DEPLOY.md §E items 7–8). M1 is deployed but not accepted (§E items 1–6). The Production real-data gate is closed (ADR 0006 §2).

The repository already settles M4's strategy: Google Calendar first through its secret iCal address with a provider-neutral `CalendarProvider` (ADR 0001 D1); read-only sync, lazy refresh on read at about 15 minutes, no background jobs (SYSTEM-ARCHITECTURE §2.4); application-level encryption of integration credentials (§5.7); external text as untrusted data (§5.5); CalendarConnection and CalendarSource deferred to M4 with credential encryption (ADR 0005 §3); imported recurrence, feed exceptions, manual single-occurrence edits and regular-week derivation in M4 (ADR 0005 §4, ADR 0006 §3); and a nonce-based script CSP before the first untrusted external content (ADR 0003 §9).

Two things in the existing documents needed correcting before M4 can be built: FAMILY-DATA-MODEL said synced records have no `created_by`, which would make a private calendar invisible even to its owner under the visibility predicate; and SYSTEM-ARCHITECTURE listed iCloud and Outlook secret addresses alongside Google's for V0.1.

## Decisions

1. **M4 development proceeds on synthetic data; real calendars stay gated.** As ADR 0006 §1: M4 is built and tested in local and Preview while M1 and M3 owner acceptance remain open. Connecting a calendar and every sync write are family-domain writes, refused in Production while `HOME_REAL_DATA` is closed. No real family calendar is connected in any environment until the gate permits it, and never in Preview.
2. **Preview test calendar (owner decision 1, approved).** A dedicated throwaway Google account owned by the owner holds a calendar of synthetic events only. Its secret address is a Preview-only credential: entered through Preview's Settings › Calendars, encrypted with Preview's key, never committed or written into an issue, PR, log or screenshot. No real family or personal data.
3. **Allowed outbound hosts (owner decision 2, modified).** M4 accepts, in Production and Preview, only the Google Calendar host and address shape required for the secret iCal feed (`https://calendar.google.com/calendar/ical/…/private-…/basic.ics`, with `webcal://` rewritten). iCloud, Outlook and any other real feed are **not** enabled in M4 merely because they serve ICS; each future real provider needs an explicit later decision. The adapter stays provider-neutral, and tests use synthetic feeds of any shape through an injected fetcher or the fake provider. No environment variable widens the allowlist in any deployed environment.
4. **Audit granularity (owner decision 4, approved).** One structural audit row per calendar refresh (`calendar.sync`), with counts only: added, changed, archived, restored, skipped, and the status. Activity is never flooded with a row per imported event. No provider text or credential ever enters audit metadata or summaries. Connection and source changes are audited one row each, structurally. CLAUDE.md rule 8 (every write audited) is met: each refresh's writes are recorded by its row.
5. **Disconnect (owner decision 3, modified).** Disconnecting does **not** hard-delete mirrored events. In one transaction: the stored credential is destroyed (ciphertext cleared), the connection is marked disconnected, its source is archived, and its mirrored events are archived. HOME-owned annotations (`event_person`) and notes are preserved. When the **same source is reliably reconnected** (the same address, recognised by its keyed fingerprint, by the same owner), the source returns and the next refresh restores and updates matching mirrored events by identity, so annotations and notes survive. Permanent purge waits for the later retention mechanism (ADR 0006 §8).
6. **Duplicate calendars (owner decision 5, modified).** HOME does not deduplicate events across distinct calendar sources because their UID or recurrence id matches: external UIDs are not assumed globally unique across unrelated sources. An exact duplicate secret address is refused when HOME can prove it (its keyed fingerprint matches a live connection); otherwise each source keeps its identity and duplicates are allowed rather than risk an incorrect merge. Cross-source fingerprinting or deduplication of events is deferred until a reliable identity mechanism exists.
7. **Attendees and organisers (owner decision 6, approved).** External attendee and organiser identities are never imported. Who is going and who is responsible stay HOME-owned (`event_person`).
8. **Sync ownership rule (correction).** A synced event has `created_by` = the calendar connection's owner, `created_via` = `sync`, and the visibility of its calendar source. The normal HOME visibility predicate then applies unchanged: a private calendar's events are its owner's alone; a household calendar's are both adults'. There is no parallel privacy system for calendar events. Sync writes run as the owner's user actor with `via: 'sync'`, minted only by the sync service, and pass through `auditedWrite` like every other write (gate, audit, Kev refusal). This corrects FAMILY-DATA-MODEL §2, which said `created_by` is null for sync.
9. **Untrusted content rule.** Imported title, description, location and any other provider text are untrusted external data. The adapter strips HTML and stores bounded plain text (the existing event limits). React's escaping and the nonce-based script CSP together form the rendering boundary. External calendar content is never treated as instructions by any part of HOME, and when Kev arrives (M8) calendar and provider text must reach it marked as untrusted context.
10. **Nonce-based script CSP first.** ADR 0003 §9's prerequisite is M4 Package 1, before any code that fetches or renders external content.
11. **Credentials.** The secret address is sealed with AES-256-GCM (Node `crypto`), a random IV, a key id, and the connection and owner ids as associated data; the key is `HOME_CREDENTIALS_KEY`, different per environment and never committed, with an optional previous key for rotation. A keyed HMAC fingerprint of the normalised address, under its own stable key (§34), recognises the same address without storing it in plain text. The address, ciphertext and fingerprint never reach the client, logs, audit, Activity, exports, error copy or LLM context. A missing key refuses calendar writes calmly and never fails boot.
12. **Refresh on use.** Rendering never writes. When a source the signed-in adult can see is older than 15 minutes, the page asks a same-origin, uncached route to refresh it without waiting; Refresh now works without JavaScript. Any adult's use may refresh a household source; a private source refreshes only on its owner's use; the writes are always the owner's. One refresh per source at a time; each refresh is one transaction, so a failure changes nothing and last-known events stay.
13. **Identity, removal and overrides.** A synced event's identity is its source, its external UID and its original occurrence (empty for a series or single event), enforced by a unique index. The same identity is the same row across refreshes. Missing from a successful feed: archived. Back: restored. New UID: new row. A moved or changed occurrence is its own row pointing at its series, with its original occurrence added to the series' exdates; a cancelled occurrence is an exdate. The same override model serves manual single-occurrence edits.
14. **Default people are derived.** A source's default people are shown for its events that have no annotations of their own; they are never written as annotations.
15. **Scope.** M4 keeps manual single-occurrence edits and regular-week derivation (already settled). It adds no calendar write, background job, authenticated provider API, cross-source deduplication, attendee import, purge, or M5–M8 feature (including the `data_health` insight: freshness is a factual line, not an insight). The one new runtime dependency is `node-ical` (approved stack).
16. **Packages and implementers.** 0 contract (Opus); 1 nonce CSP (Opus); 2 credentials and safe fetch (Opus); 3 provider layer and recurrence extension (Opus); 4a migration only (Opus); 4b services and sync (Opus); 5 Settings › Calendars (Fable); 6 synced events across screens (Fable); 7 regular week (Opus); 8a single-occurrence engine and service (Opus); 8b its screens (Fable); 9 acceptance and audit (Opus). Packages are acceptance boundaries and may be split into smaller PRs. Migration-first: 4a merges and runs in Production and Preview before 4b or 8a merges.

### Implementation clarifications, Package 1 (for review with its PR)

17. **The nonce-based script CSP.** `src/proxy.ts` runs on every request except Next's hashed build assets (`/_next/static`, `/_next/image`): it makes a nonce (128 random bits from Web Crypto, base64; never logged, never stored, never reused) and sets the policy from `src/lib/csp.ts` on the request, where Next.js reads the nonce while rendering and puts it on its own scripts, and on the response. The root layout awaits `connection()`, so every page renders per request and no prerendered page can ship scripts without a nonce; only `robots.txt` and the two icons stay static. The production policy is `default-src 'self'; script-src 'nonce-…' 'strict-dynamic'; style-src 'self' 'nonce-…'; img-src 'self'; font-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'; manifest-src 'self'; media-src 'none'; worker-src 'none'; frame-src 'none'; object-src 'none'; base-uri 'self'; form-action 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'; upgrade-insecure-requests`. HOME loads nothing from another origin, so no other origin appears; no calendar host is added (calendar fetching is server-side, Packages 2–3). `next dev` adds only `'unsafe-eval'` (React's development stacks) and leaves out `upgrade-insecure-requests` (the dev server is plain http); its development tools also add unnonced `<style>` tags, which the dev-server tests set aside by their source and which `next start` never serves. Inline style attributes are refused (no `'unsafe-inline'` anywhere), so `global-error.tsx` now uses classes and its own stylesheet. The other security headers stay in `next.config.ts`, unchanged. No CSP report endpoint (none existed). `scripts/check-csp.mts` checks the production policy against `next start` in CI; `tests/e2e/csp.spec.ts` checks headers, nonces, ordinary use without violations or hydration complaints, refusal of injected unnonced markup, and that hostile calendar-style text renders as words.

### Implementation clarifications, Package 2 (for review with its PR)

18. **Credentials at rest.** `src/trust/credentials.ts`. `HOME_CREDENTIALS_KEY` is exactly 32 random bytes in canonical standard base64 (44 characters); anything else, a placeholder or one repeated byte is `key_invalid`. `src/lib/env.ts` accepts any string so a wrong value never fails boot; `credentialKeysFrom` turns the raw values into `ready`, `absent` or `invalid` (naming the variable, never the value), and the boot logs that one word. Two subkeys come from the key by HKDF-SHA-256 (`home/credentials/aes-256-gcm/v1`, `home/credentials/address-fingerprint/v1`). A sealed credential is `hc1.<key id>.<iv>.<ciphertext>.<tag>` in canonical base64url: the format version, a 16-hex key id (SHA-256 over a label and the key), a fresh 12-byte IV, AES-256-GCM ciphertext and its 16-byte tag; the associated data is the JSON array of a fixed label, the connection id and the owner's user id, so a sealed value opens only for the row and owner it was made for. Any other key, row, format, malformed part, non-canonical spelling or changed bit is `credential_unreadable`. Plaintext is 1–4096 bytes. `HOME_CREDENTIALS_KEY_PREVIOUS`, during a rotation only, opens what it sealed; nothing new is sealed with it (`sealedWithPreviousKey` says what to reseal). The address fingerprint is `fp1.` plus HMAC-SHA-256 of the normalised address under the fingerprint subkey: stable for every accepted spelling of one address under one key, different under another key, and never used to compare events or sources. Every error is a `CredentialError` whose message is its code.
19. **Accepted addresses.** `src/lib/calendar-address.ts` (pure, reachable by the domain layer). Only `https://calendar.google.com/calendar/ical/<calendar id>/private-<token>/basic.ics`, with `webcal://` rewritten, case and the default port normalised, surrounding whitespace trimmed; refused with a code: any other scheme (including protocol-relative and scheme-less input), user or password (including an empty one, in the `webcal` form too: the authority is read from the raw input in both schemes), any other host (subdomain tricks, trailing dot, IP literals in any notation, homoglyphs), another port, any query or fragment (even empty), another path shape (including encoded traversal), backslashes, whitespace or control characters inside, and input over 2048 characters. The normal form is the scheme, host and path only. `isApprovedCalendarUrl` judges each redirect hop: https, the approved host, the default port, no credentials.
20. **Safe outbound fetch.** `src/integrations/net/safe-fetch.ts`, Node's own `https` (no dependency). `safeGet(address, { policy })` makes GET requests only, with fixed `Accept`, `Accept-Encoding: gzip, identity` and `User-Agent` headers and nothing else (no cookie, credential or HOME header; no connection reuse). The production policy, `googleCalendarPolicy` (frozen), approves only Google's host over https and only public addresses; a test passes its own policy in code, and no environment value widens it. Every name is resolved through a lookup hook at the moment of connecting, and if any resolved address is not public the request is refused (`forbidden_destination`), which also defeats a name that resolves differently later; the hook (`guardLookup`) answers in the shape Node asks for, the whole judged list when it asks for all addresses (its default, as it tries IPv6 and IPv4 in turn) and one address otherwise (Opus review of PR #43, blocker: returning one address to an `all` request meant no hostname ever connected); the connected socket's address is checked again, which covers IP literals that skip the lookup. `src/lib/net-policy.ts` defines public: IPv4 outside loopback, private, CGNAT, link-local and metadata, documentation, benchmarking, multicast and reserved ranges; IPv6 in global unicast outside documentation, Teredo, 6to4, NAT64 and the special 2001::/23 block, with every IPv4-mapped form refused. Redirects are followed by hand, at most three, each judged by the policy before any request is made to it. The whole fetch, every hop included, has 10 seconds; the body may be 5 MB after decompression (gzip only; any other encoding is `bad_response`), checked against the declared length and while reading. Statuses: 401, 403, 404 and 410 are `address_rejected`; any other non-200 and any network failure are `unreachable`. A body HOME does not read (a redirect, an error status, a refusal) is never drained: the response and request are destroyed as soon as the outcome is known, so nothing keeps downloading after the fetch settles (review finding). The result is `{ text, bytes, contentType }`. Errors are `SafeFetchError` with a code only and no cause; nothing in the module logs.
21. **Logs.** The redaction list gains the words for credentials (`address`, `credential(s)`, `ciphertext`, `sealed`, `fingerprint`, `nonce`, `key`), and any field whose name contains `credential`, `secret`, `ciphertext` or `password` is redacted wherever the word sits (a field named after the future `credentials_encrypted` column was the gap the tests found). `sanitiseMessage` also removes base64 runs containing `+`, `/` or `=`. Beyond names, every string the logger writes (values at any depth, array items, keys, error names and messages) is scrubbed by content (`scrubSecrets`): any URL or bare path through Google's calendar export or carrying a `private-` token becomes `[calendar-address]`, a sealed credential (`hc<n>.` and four parts) becomes `[credential]`, a fingerprint (`fp<n>.`) becomes `[fingerprint]`, and the words around them are kept. `location`, `href`, `uri` and `link` join the names, and any name containing `sealed` or `feed` is redacted (review finding: a secret address under a generic name or in free text was logged in full).

### Implementation clarifications, Package 3 (for review with its PR)

22. **The provider boundary.** The provider-neutral types and the `CalendarProvider` interface live in `src/domain/calendar/provider.ts`, so the sync service (Package 4b) depends on them without importing an adapter; `src/integrations/calendar/` implements them. `ExternalEvent` is `uid`, `recurrenceId` (null, or the original occurrence: an ISO date for an all-day series, a UTC instant `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` for a timed one), `status` (`confirmed` | `tentative`), `time` (timed instants with an IANA zone, or all-day dates with an exclusive end), `rrule`, `recurrence` (`none` | `rule` | `unreadable`), `exdates`, `cancelledOccurrences`, sanitised `title`, `description`, `location`, and the provider's `sequence` and `updatedAt` (never identity). Nothing else: no attendees, organiser, alarms, attachments, URLs, conferencing or raw properties. Cancelled events and cancelled occurrences never appear as events (§26). `fetchEvents` also returns the calendar, `skipped`, `feedHash` (§29) and `notes`, counts of what was read with care (`unreadableRecurrence`, `unknownZone`, `duplicate`, `ignoredRdate`, `orphanOverride`), never content. A provider fails only with `CalendarProviderError` and one of the §3.8 codes (`unreachable`, `address_rejected`, `not_a_calendar`, `too_large`), its message the code. The adapter logs nothing. `listCalendars` reads the feed (an ICS address has no other way to show it works) and returns one calendar, `default`, with the feed's own name as plain text.
23. **node-ical, and what HOME reads itself** (the one new runtime dependency, `node-ical` 0.27.3, approved stack). node-ical is the parser of record for the format: line unfolding, property and parameter syntax, quoting and TEXT unescaping. It is not trusted with time: it resolves zones by guessing (a VTIMEZONE's offsets, the server's own zone for floating times, an unknown zone read as UTC), folds overrides into their series by date, and throws for the whole feed when one event repeats DTSTART. So the whole feed is RFC 5545-unfolded first (§32), split into its top-level VEVENTs, and each is parsed alone (one bad event is skipped and counted; events sharing a UID are never merged); node-ical receives an allowlist only: UID, SUMMARY, DESCRIPTION, LOCATION, STATUS and SEQUENCE as they are, and DTSTART, DTEND, DURATION, RRULE, EXRULE, EXDATE, RDATE, RECURRENCE-ID, LAST-MODIFIED, DTSTAMP and CREATED renamed `X-HOME-…`, which it keeps verbatim with their parameters for HOME to interpret; every other property and every nested component (alarms) is dropped before parsing. VTIMEZONE and every component other than VEVENT are never read. A feed must begin with `BEGIN:VCALENDAR` and end with `END:VCALENDAR`, so a cut-off body is `not_a_calendar` and can never read as deleted events.
24. **Times and zones** (`src/lib/time-zones.ts`, `normalise.ts`). A TZID is used when it is an IANA name (in its canonical spelling) or a Windows name in a fixed table taken from CLDR's windowsZones for territory 001 (32 names); offsets, abbreviations, custom and VTIMEZONE-defined names are unknown. An unknown zone, and a time with no zone (floating), is read in the calendar's `X-WR-TIMEZONE` when that is an IANA name, else `HOME_TIMEZONE`; an unknown zone is counted. A `Z` time is UTC and keeps `UTC` as its zone. Wall clocks become instants by the engine's rules (a skipped hour moves forward, a repeated hour takes the first instance). A start in the spring-forward gap is moved forward by the gap and the event keeps the length its source gave it: with both ends local in one zone, their wall-clock difference read before the gap is resolved (02:30–03:30 on the NZ spring-forward night becomes 03:30–04:30 NZDT, never 03:30–03:30); with the end in another zone, the end moves by the gap too. A start that exists keeps the real elapsed time to its end, so 01:30–03:30 across the change lasts one hour; fall-back is unchanged (02:30–03:30 takes the first 02:30 and lasts two hours). Only a source that is itself zero-length (no end, or DTEND equal to DTSTART) is zero-length in HOME. All-day events are dates with an exclusive end; a missing end, or an end equal to the start, is one day. A timed event without DTEND uses DURATION (weeks and days on the wall clock, hours, minutes and seconds elapsed), else the start. An event that cannot be read is skipped and counted: no UID, a missing or repeated DTSTART, an end before the start, a date start with a time end, both DTEND and DURATION, a value that is not a date (including a quoted `tzone://` TZID, which the line syntax cannot carry), and any date outside 1900–2199, HOME's own date range.
25. **Recurrence.** A provider's RRULE is kept as written; the only change is its UNTIL, spelled as RFC 5545 asks for that event (a date for all-day; UTC for timed, a floating UNTIL being read in the event's zone; an all-day series' time-of-day UNTIL, as Outlook writes it, becoming its date in the calendar's zone). A rule is read when it is one RRULE of at most 1000 characters with FREQ DAILY, WEEKLY, MONTHLY or YEARLY, no BYHOUR, BYMINUTE or BYSECOND, no repeated part, not both COUNT and UNTIL, and the engine can build it. Anything else, an EXRULE (which would remove occurrences HOME could not see), or an EXDATE that cannot be read, leaves the event as its first occurrence only (`recurrence: 'unreadable'`, counted): HOME never invents occurrences. RDATE is not read (no occurrence is added) and is counted. The recurrence engine gains, with manual behaviour unchanged: `isReadableRRule`; a rule that parses but cannot be built (an unknown weekday code) now reads as unreadable instead of throwing during expansion, which also protects stored manual rules; a timed UNTIL with a time of day stops at that instant (HOME's own rules end at the last second of the chosen day, so they read exactly as before); and `occursWithin`, a window check that stops at the first occurrence and walks at most a given number of steps.
26. **Overrides and cancellations.** An event's identity is its UID and its original occurrence. An override's identity (`recurrenceId`) is its RECURRENCE-ID as written and never depends on its series: a DATE is an ISO date and a DATE-TIME the UTC instant its own zone (or the calendar's floating zone) names, so an override reads the same whether or not its series is in the feed. Only the exdate added to a series is in the series' terms: a date for an all-day series, a UTC instant for a timed one (a date naming an occurrence of a timed series is the series' own time that day, in its zone). A moved or changed occurrence is its own event, `rrule` empty, and its original joins the series' `exdates`; a cancelled occurrence (STATUS:CANCELLED with RECURRENCE-ID) joins `exdates` and `cancelledOccurrences` and is not an event. Originals are added for a single event too, which the engine honours. A cancelled series is removed with its overrides. `RANGE=THISANDFUTURE` rewrites the rest of a series, which HOME does not apply, so that override is skipped and counted. An override whose series is not in the feed is kept when it moves an occurrence and dropped when it cancels one, and counted (`orphanOverride`); no parent is made up for it. **Package 4a** must therefore let an override row carry its original occurrence with no parent (a nullable parent reference); its identity stays source, UID and recurrence id, and **Package 4b** may link it to its series when the series appears. The same identity twice keeps the latest revision (SEQUENCE, then LAST-MODIFIED, then a fixed order of content), whatever order the feed lists them in, and is counted. A series with more than 500 exdates (the event schema's limit) is read as unreadable; the feed's EXDATE values are counted before any is read, so a huge list is refused without converting it.
27. **Untrusted text** (`src/integrations/calendar/text.ts`). Title, location and details, and the calendar's own name, become plain text: input over 200,000 characters is cut first; lone surrogate halves become U+FFFD; script, style, iframe, object, template, noscript, svg and math elements go with their content; comments go; line-breaking tags become line breaks and list items bullets; every other tag goes with its attributes (so no handler or `javascript:` link survives as markup); entities are decoded once (`&lt;script&gt;` stays the words "<script>", which React escapes when shown); text is NFC-normalised; control characters other than line breaks, bidi controls, zero-width and other invisible formatting characters and the BOM are removed; titles and places become one line; details keep at most one blank line; each is bounded to the event limits (title 200, place 500, details 10,000) with an ellipsis that never splits a character; nothing readable left is null. Text is never turned into a link or URL, never treated as an instruction and never sent to an LLM.
28. **Window and limits.** `importWindow(today)` is 30 days before to 400 days after today in the home zone. An event is kept when any occurrence starts in the window (with a day's margin each side for zones, and its own length before it, so a long event reaching in counts); a series is kept whole, overrides included, when any part reaches it. The contract's 5,000 is applied to these relevant events (series, single events and overrides with an occurrence in the window), not to every VEVENT, because Google's feed carries the calendar's whole history and a long-lived calendar would otherwise be refused; the 5 MB cap bounds the rest. Over 5,000 is `too_large`. Imported rules are walked from their start, so the work is bounded: a series that needs more than 20,000 steps to reach the window is read as unreadable (counted), and a feed needing more than 300,000 in all is `too_large` (measured at about 4.4 µs a step, about 1.3 s at worst). The rrule library checks UNTIL only when a date passes the rule's filters, so a rule that never yields (the 30th of February, every day) was searched to the year 9999, about 6 seconds each; every expansion and window check now stops searching at the end of its query (§32). **Long-running recurrence must stay bounded before synced events reach Today, Forward or Coming up** (owner decision): a series is still walked from its start on every render, and Packages 4b and 6 must bound that (for example by the import-time step limit, a stored horizon, or re-anchoring) before synced events are shown. Package 2's fetch limits are unchanged.
29. **The feed hash.** `feedHash` is `h1:` and the SHA-256 of the feed *as HOME reads it* (the calendar, the normalised events in order, `skipped` and `notes`), not of the raw bytes: Google writes a fresh DTSTAMP on every event each time it serves the feed, so raw bytes never repeat. The hash changes exactly when something HOME keeps changes, so Package 4b can skip every write when it matches; it can also change when the moving window brings an event in or out.
30. **Fake provider, corpus and contract suite.** `src/integrations/calendar/fake.ts` replays a script of steps (a synthetic ICS feed read by exactly the adapter's parser and normaliser, events already in HOME's shape, or a failure code); the current step answers until a test calls `advance()` or `goTo()`; it approves the address as the adapter does, never touches the network, and is never used outside tests. `tests/fixtures/calendars/` holds a Google-shaped feed builder (folded lines, DTSTAMP churn, VTIMEZONE, attendees, organiser, alarms, conferencing and attachments on invited events), the fifteen scenario sequences (initial, unchanged, added, changed, moved, cancelled occurrence, deleted, returning, same UID changed, recreated with a new UID, series with overrides, NZ DST, floating times, malformed, hostile text) and Outlook- and iCloud-shaped feeds for robustness only. `tests/unit/calendar/provider-contract.ts` is the contract suite every provider must pass (one calendar; stable identity; deterministic and order-independent results and hash; timed and all-day shapes; rules kept; overrides and cancellations; zones and DST; plain, bounded text; no attendee or organiser data; skipped events counted; structural errors only; the address approved before anything is read; nothing logged); it runs against the adapter and the fake.
31. **Package 2 carry-forward.** The logger now scrubs its event argument (the message) with the same value-based scrubber as every other string, so a secret passed as the message cannot leak; fixed event names are written unchanged. `pnpm verify` runs the whole local verification one step at a time and keeps each step's full output in `verify-logs/` (gitignored), printing a failed step's failing tests and the end of its output (review of PR #43: a failure kept as one summary line could not be diagnosed afterwards).

32. **Fixes from the Opus review of PR #44.** (a) *Unfolding before isolation:* RFC 5545 lets a line fold inside a property name, so renaming before unfolding let a folded EXDATE, RRULE or RECURRENCE-ID reach node-ical's own date handling (a folded EXDATE was silently lost, showing an occurrence the calendar had removed); the feed is now unfolded first and node-ical given only the allowlist of §23. (b) *Spring-forward gap:* a start in the gap no longer collapses the event (§24). (c) *Override identity:* independent of the series (§26). (d) *Bounded work:* expansion and window checks stop the rrule library's search at the end of the query, by answering its one per-step exit check (`options.interval === 0`) once the steps needed to pass the query's end are spent; results up to that end are unchanged (tested against an unlimited window), and the method is tied to rrule 2.8's iteration, which the tests would show if an upgrade changed it. EXDATE values are counted before any is read. (e) *Invisible characters:* the untrusted-text filter's own pattern held literal bidi overrides (escapes decoded when the file was written); every such character in `src/` and `tests/` is now a visible escape, and `tests/unit/invisible-characters.test.ts` fails on any literal invisible or bidi-control character there (Trojan Source).

### Implementation clarifications, Package 4a (for review with its PR)

33. **The calendar schema (migration `0007`, migration only).** Exact columns and constraints are in FAMILY-DATA-MODEL; the decisions are:
    - **`calendar_connection`** is owner-only (no visibility column), `owner_user_id` → `user` `RESTRICT`. The plain calendar address never enters the database: `credentials_encrypted` and `address_fingerprint` accept only Package 2's shapes (`hc1.<16 hex>.<three base64url parts>`, ≤ 8192 characters; `fp1.<base64url>`), so a CHECK refuses an address stored there by mistake. State is one CHECK: `active` has a credential and key id and no `disconnected_at`; `disconnected` has neither and a `disconnected_at`. Disconnecting therefore destroys the credential and keeps the row and its fingerprint, which is how the same owner's reconnection is recognised (§5). One live connection per fingerprint (partial unique index over `status = 'active'`), whoever made it. Error codes are structural (`^[a-z][a-z_]{0,39}$`), never provider text.

### Implementation clarifications, Package 4b (for review with its PR)

34. **A stable fingerprint key (carry-forward from the 4a review).** Package 2 derived the address fingerprint from `HOME_CREDENTIALS_KEY`, so rotating that key would change every fingerprint; a disconnected calendar keeps only its fingerprint (its address is destroyed), so after a rotation it could never be recognised again and reconnecting would make a new calendar beside the old archived one. Fingerprints now use **`HOME_FINGERPRINT_KEY`**: separate key material in the same format (exactly 32 random bytes, canonical standard base64), its HMAC subkey derived with HKDF-SHA-256 (`home/calendar/address-fingerprint/v2`), written `fp2.<HMAC-SHA-256, base64url>` (migration 0007's CHECK already accepts any `fpN`, so no migration). It is separate per environment, never equal to a credentials key (refused as `invalid`), and **stable**: rotating `HOME_CREDENTIALS_KEY` never touches it; it changes only if it may have leaked, and then disconnected calendars are no longer recognised. Missing or invalid, it refuses connecting calendars (`calendar_keys_unavailable`) and never stops HOME booting; boot logs its state, never its value. `fp1` (Package 2's derivation) was never stored and is removed. The sealing key still rotates as Package 2 designed: a credential sealed with `HOME_CREDENTIALS_KEY_PREVIOUS` opens, and the sync service **reseals it with the current key on its next refresh**, so DEPLOY.md §D's rotation is: rotate, refresh every calendar, check that live connections use one key id, remove the previous key. Both keys are read from the environment on each use (`calendarKeyEnv`, like the gate), so a rotation applies without a restart.
35. **The external UID is bounded in UTF-8 bytes (carry-forward).** The synced-identity index holds the UID itself and cannot store an entry over about 2,700 bytes, so a valid 1,000-character UID of three-byte characters would have failed a refresh's whole write. `MAX_EXTERNAL_UID_BYTES = 512` (`src/domain/calendar/provider.ts`, `uidFits`): the adapter skips and counts a longer UID, `finish` applies the same rule to any provider's events, and the sync service checks again before writing. A UID is never truncated, since truncation could merge two identities. No migration: the bound lives at the provider and service boundary, where an over-long UID is a skipped event, not a database error.
36. **Connections, sources and the sync invariants** (`src/domain/calendar/service.ts`, `mirror.ts`). A calendar's handle everywhere outside the module is its **source id**; the connection's id, credential, key id and fingerprint never leave the module. Connecting, changing, disconnecting and refreshing are a person's own acts (`via: 'ui'`): Kev (`kev_cannot_write`), the system (`not_a_user`) and the sync actor (`sync_actor`) are refused. Connect is gated first, then the address is normalised and approved (`address_not_accepted` otherwise), fingerprinted, refused if that fingerprint is already **live** whoever owns it (`calendar_already_connected`; the live-fingerprint index settles a race), and sealed bound to the row and its owner; the source starts with `created_by` = the owner and `created_via` = `ui`. Only the owner changes a calendar (`not_owner`); a change of visibility applies to its synced events in the same transaction, under the reference rules (not private while household notes point at its events; not household while its events name private people), and so does a change of usual kind. **Every synced write** is checked by `assertMirrored` before the database sees it: created by the connection's owner, via sync, `source = synced`, in its own source, with its source's visibility, and a parent only when it has an original, never itself, and only the same UID's series in the same source. A breach rolls the refresh back. The sync actor (`issueSyncActor`, the owner's user actor with `via: 'sync'`) is minted only by the sync service and refused by every person's service; a hand-made `via: 'sync'` actor is not one (`tests/unit/execution-guard.test.ts`). Manual override rules are unchanged (Package 8a).
37. **Sync transaction, concurrency, failure and audit** (`src/domain/calendar/sync.ts`). A refresh is one `auditedWrite` as the sync actor, so the gate, the Kev refusal and the audit apply. Inside it: `pg_try_advisory_xact_lock` on the source (a second refresh of the same source returns `busy` at once and writes nothing; a different source refreshes alongside; no in-memory lock, so it holds across servers), the source and its connection locked and checked (active, same owner), the credential opened for this one fetch only (`calendar_credential_unreadable` if HOME's keys no longer open it) and the provider called. The transaction is open during the fetch (Package 2 bounds it at 10 seconds), which is what makes a refresh atomic and the lock real. **A provider failure** (one of §3.8's four codes) records the status and code on the source and connection, writes one audit row, and changes no event: last-known events stay. Any other error rolls everything back. **An unchanged feed** (the same feed hash after an `ok` or `partial` refresh) moves only the freshness. **Otherwise** the feed is applied by identity: series first, so overrides can point at them; new identities inserted, changed ones (content tag `external_etag` = `e1:` + SHA-256 of the provider-owned fields, or a changed parent, visibility, kind or owner) updated in place, archived ones restored and updated, identities missing from the feed archived (never deleted). A row keeps its id across every change, so `event_person` and notes survive. An override is linked to its series when the series is in the feed and stored with no parent otherwise; its identity never depends on the link. Provider-owned fields: title (`Untitled` when the calendar gives none), details, place, time, zone, all-day, rule, exdates, UID, original occurrence and the content tag; `tentative` is read but not stored (no column; it shows as confirmed). Kind is the source's (else `other`), `domain` empty. **Freshness:** `last_attempt_at` on every refresh, `last_synced_at`, `feed_hash` and `last_skipped_count` on success, status `ok` or `partial` (anything skipped) or the failure code. **Audit:** one `calendar.sync` row per refresh about the source (so it follows the calendar's visibility), with the status, `unchanged` and the counts `added`, `changed`, `archived`, `restored`, `skipped`; actor the owner `via sync`, whoever caused it. Connections are audited as `calendar.connect`, `calendar.reconnect` and `calendar.disconnect` about the connection (registered owner-only: the other adult never sees them); sources as `calendar_source.create`, `.update` (field names), `.archive` (count of events archived) and `.restore`. Event names do not repeat the connection's table name, so only the calendar module and the Activity registry ever name it. No metadata holds a name, title, place, UID, address, credential, key id or fingerprint. Refresh-on-use (`refreshStaleCalendars`) refreshes each visible calendar older than 15 minutes, once, reporting refusals rather than retrying.
38. **Export, version 2.** The format moves to **version 2**, adding `calendars` (the calendar's id, name, visibility, usual kind and people, freshness and status, and the common fields) and the events' `recurrenceParentId` and `recurrenceOriginal`. `calendar_connection` stays excluded; `calendar_source.connectionId`, `externalCalendarId` and `feedHash` are excluded with reasons. The completeness guard gains a **tripwire**: no exclusion may describe itself as temporary ("until", "pending", "later", "package"…), `calendar_source` must be exported, and secret columns never. An adult's export holds the calendars they can see (their own, and household ones), read through the calendar service.
39. **The agenda bound** (§28's owner decision). `expandEvent` no longer walks a rule from its DTSTART: `anchorFor` restarts it at the start of one of its own periods (day, week by WKST, month or year, stepped by INTERVAL from DTSTART) two periods before the window, with every part the rule took from DTSTART (weekdays, nth weekdays, month days, months, time of day) written out, so the occurrences from there are identical (the margin is defensive: rrule 2.8 keeps a period's dates inside it, so restarting at the window's own period gives the same result, which the mutation proof records as an equivalent mutant; restarting after the window's start, or dropping a written-out part, fails the reference tests); never with COUNT (numbered from DTSTART, and already bounded by `MAX_COUNT`), never near DTSTART. The work is then proportional to the window, not the series' age (a daily series from 1950 read for a week: 7 occurrences, under 5 ms). `tests/unit/calendar/rrule-reference.test.ts` compares it with plain expansion from DTSTART for 33 rules, four old starts and six far windows. `occursWithin` (the import's step budget, §28) is unchanged. **What Package 6 must use:** the agenda engine and its loader as they are, which call `expandEvent` with the requested window; nothing may expand a synced series by any other path (no `RRule` built from a stored rule outside the recurrence engine), and no stored horizon or cache is needed.
40. **Reconnect** (as amended by §42). Connecting an address whose fingerprint matches one of **your own** disconnected calendars is refused (`calendar_can_reconnect`) and changes nothing. `reconnectCalendar(actor, calendarId, { address })` takes the address and nothing else (a strict schema, so settings sent to it are refused rather than ignored): the address's fingerprint must equal the one that calendar's connection kept (`calendar_address_mismatch` otherwise; `not_owner` for anyone else's; `calendar_already_connected` if it is live). The same connection and calendar return with their own settings, the credential is resealed for the same row, the feed hash is cleared so the next refresh applies the feed in full and restores the events by identity with their people and notes. Its default people are checked again: any that is gone, no longer visible to the owner, or private while the calendar is household is dropped, and the restore's audit row counts how many. Concurrent reconnects: one succeeds, the other is `calendar_already_connected`. Another adult can never reconnect your calendar; they may connect the same address as a calendar of their own.
41. **Refresh-on-use composition** (as amended by §42). `src/integrations/calendar/entry.ts` is the calendar composition root: the one module that assembles the domain's refresh operations with the real `ics` provider (integrations may import domain), exposing only provider-free operations, `refreshCalendarNow(actor, id)` and `refreshStaleCalendarsNow(actor)`. App may import that exact module and nothing else in integrations: a single, regex-scoped exception in `eslint.config.mjs`, held by `tests/unit/layer-exceptions.test.ts` (which runs ESLint to prove every other integrations import is refused). The domain still never imports integrations, and app never sees a provider. `POST /calendars/refresh` (contract §2.2's refresh-on-use route) calls it: same-origin, signed in, `no-store`, answering only `{ changed }`; a refusal (the gate, Kev, missing keys) is "no change", anything unexpected is logged as its class name only. Package 5's Refresh now will call `refreshCalendarNow`.

45. **The regular week (Package 7).** A person's profile shows `Usually`: their weekly and fortnightly series, by weekday, as a small household timetable in the agenda's own rows. It is derived on read by the `profile` engine (`regularWeek`, `src/domain/engines/profile.ts`) from exactly the events the agenda loader just read as the signed-in adult, with exactly its people (§44); nothing is stored, nothing is inferred about the person (CLAUDE.md rule 11), and no screen filters anything.
    - **What qualifies.** A series counts for a person when they are among its effective people, in either role (contract §3.6: "attends or is responsible for"; FAMILY-DATA-MODEL §3): its own annotations, else its calendar's usual people. Never from a title, description, organiser, attendee, owner or place. Its rule must be a plain weekly rhythm (`weeklyCadence`, `src/domain/engines/recurrence.ts`): `FREQ=WEEKLY` with `INTERVAL` 1 or 2, plain weekdays (no "second Tuesday"), and nothing else beyond `COUNT`, `UNTIL` and `WKST` (which calendars write and which only says where a week starts). One-offs, daily, monthly (by date or by ordinal weekday), yearly (birthdays), every third week or more, rules with any other part (`BYHOUR`, `BYSETPOS`, `BYMONTH` …) and unreadable rules are left out, never approximated: if HOME cannot say it plainly, it says nothing. The series must also have an occurrence in its next cycle (seven days from today, or fourteen for a fortnightly one), so an ended series or one that has not begun is not part of the week.
    - **The rhythm is the rule's.** The cycle is expanded by the recurrence engine without the series' exdates, so a skipped or cancelled date (or an override's original) does not hide the rhythm; a moved occurrence is its own row with no rule, so it never makes one. Archived events and archived overrides are not read at all.
    - **Weekday and time are the home zone's.** Each occurrence in the cycle is placed on its home-zone date (`isoDateInZone`) and given its home-zone wall clock (`clockOf`); the weekday shown is the home weekday, which can differ from the event's own (a Tuesday-evening London series is a Wednesday-morning one in Auckland). The time is that of the next occurrence on that weekday, so across a DST change it follows the engine (an Auckland series keeps its wall clock; a New York one moves by an hour at home while the two zones disagree). No regular-week time logic exists outside the engine.
    - **Fortnightly is never flattened.** A fortnightly series is one entry per weekday with `Every second Wednesday` under its title; a weekly one has no such line.
    - **All-day series** (not settled by the contract; this package's decision): a weekly or fortnightly all-day series counts only when each occurrence is a single day, shown as `All day` with no time; a multi-day one is left out.
    - **Order.** Monday to Sunday, all-day before timed, then by home-zone time, title and event id, whatever the order of the input.
    - **On the profile.** `Usually` sits between Things to know and Coming up: a weekday heading, then one row per entry linking to the event's own page. No section at all when there is nothing regular (no empty state), and no edit control, source label, confidence or count.
    - **Package 6 carry-forward fixed.** `effectivePeople` now decides whether an event has people of its own before leaving anyone out for display: an event whose only annotation names someone the reader no longer lists (an archived person) shows nobody, never its calendar's usual people instead. No privacy rule changed.
    - **Carried forward.** Package 9: the loader's one-query-per-event annotations, the duplicate calendar read in the stale check, and the household event read on a synced event's page (§44); the regular week adds no query of its own.
