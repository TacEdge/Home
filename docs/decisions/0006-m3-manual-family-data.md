# ADR 0006 — M3 manual family data + capture: scope, gate and decisions

Status: **Accepted**, 2026-10-04 (owner approved the M3 plan, decisions 1–5 and the settled items). Contract: `docs/m3/M3-BUILD-CONTRACT.md`.

## Context

M2 is complete (ADR 0005, closed by PR #29). M1 is deployed to production but not accepted: Postmark live sending, signed-in production checks, live rate-limit verification and the Neon recovery condition remain open (`docs/runbooks/DEPLOY.md` §E). ADR 0005 §1 gated "M3 real-user and real-data work" on those items. The owner wants M3 to make HOME visibly usable, and has clarified that the gate applies to real household data in Production, not to development on synthetic data.

## Decisions

1. **M3 development proceeds now; real data stays gated.** M3 is built and tested on synthetic data in local and Preview. Real household data enters Production only when the real-data gate opens (§2). This refines ADR 0005 §1 (D-M2-1): the gate is on *real data*, not on building M3.
2. **Production real-data gate (owner decision 1).** In Production every family-domain write is refused unless `HOME_REAL_DATA` is exactly `open`; missing, blank, malformed or any other value keeps it closed (fail-closed). Authentication, auth audit, Activity, boot guards and other infrastructure stay operational, and an unexpected value never fails boot. The gate does not apply outside Production, so local and Preview development continues. The owner sets the value only after every DEPLOY.md §E item, the M3 restore rehearsal and the export check are recorded. Contract §6.
3. **Recurrence in M3 (owner decision 2).** A limited model, built by the `recurrence` engine with the `rrule` dependency: none, daily, weekly on chosen days, fortnightly, monthly on the same date, yearly; ends never, on a date or after N times; whole-series editing; **skip this one** via exdate. Single-occurrence edits, imported-calendar recurrence and richer exception handling (RECURRENCE-ID overrides, feed EXDATEs) stay in M4, which extends this engine. Regular-week derivation stays in M4 (ADR 0005 §4). This moves the recurrence engine's first version from the M4 roadmap line into M3. Contract §5.
4. **Plain Forward in M3 (owner decision 3).** `/forward` is a factual 30-day agenda grouped by day. No conflict, coordination or Week Ahead intelligence is brought forward from M6.
5. **Accessible Mist (owner decision 4).** Amends ADR 0004. Mist as specified (`#8C9C95` day, `#7F8D88` night) is about 2.6:1 on Morning and 2.3:1 on Linen by day, and 4.3:1 on Linen at night, below WCAG AA (4.5:1) for the small labels and times the brand assigns it. Text uses an accessible Mist: **`#5A6B64` by day** (5.1:1 on Morning, 4.6:1 on Linen) and **`#8E9B96` at night** (5.9:1 on night ground, 5.1:1 on night Linen). The original Mist is kept for decorative, non-text marks only (hairline accents, load dots that are never the only signal). In code, `--muted` becomes the accessible text value and a new `--muted-mark` holds the original; Package 1 makes the change with a contrast unit test. `docs/BRAND.md` records the mapping.
6. **Export (settled).** Each adult exports their own authorised view as versioned JSON (`home-export`, version 1): household records, their own private records, captures, proposals, conversations, insight responses and their own Activity. The other adult's private records never appear. Sensitive context is included only when the person explicitly ticks **Include sensitive items**, through the audited sensitive read. No import in M3. A completeness guard fails when a column is neither exported nor deliberately excluded. Contract §7.1.
7. **Recovery before real data (owner decision 5).** Neon point-in-time restore with ≥ 7 days on `home`, plus a successful, dated restore rehearsal (end to end on `home-dev`; branch restore and verification on `home`), plus the versioned JSON export, is sufficient before real family data. No scheduled dump in M3. Contract §7.2.
8. **Purge deferred (owner decision 5).** Automated purge execution (30-day archived and dismissed captures, 90-day conversations) is deferred; real data may enter before it exists. Until it does, UI and copy must never imply that archived or dismissed records have been permanently deleted. The retention model of ADR 0005 §6 and §43 is unchanged.
9. **No schema changes planned.** M2's schema holds everything M3 uses: `rrule` and `exdates`, `origin_capture_id`, capture statuses, context sensitivity and status, `person.user_id`, the P-1 audit columns. The gate is an environment variable; export and restore need no tables. A migration found necessary later is a contract amendment and follows migration-first (ADR 0005 §16).
10. **Full pages now, sheets later (settled).** Every detail has its own URL; M3 renders full pages. The concept's sheets are layered over the same URLs later (M5/M6) without changing routes.
11. **Notes live on a subject; manual organisation through the executor; capture wording (settled).** The M3 UI creates notes only on a project, person or event; a free-floating thought is a capture. Organising a capture by hand creates a proposal as the person and approves it in one transaction, so the existing executor and capture settlement run unchanged and provenance is `ui` (ADR 0005 §43); no second organising path exists. The capture bar reads "Tell HOME something…" until Kev arrives in M8.
12. **Kev transcript and usage recording moves to M8 (settled).** The open question in ADR 0005 §39 (how Kev's orchestrator records its own transcript and usage) is decided with the orchestrator in M8, not before M3. M3 adds no Kev write.
13. **Packages are acceptance boundaries (owner rule).** A work package may be split into smaller PRs when its size warrants, without changing its approved architecture or scope. Every PR is owner-merged.
14. **Fixture seeding stays local only (settled).** `home-dev` and Production are never seeded; Preview data is synthetic and entered through the UI.
15. **Implementers.** Opus for Packages 0, 1, 2, 4, 7 and 10; Fable for 3, 5, 6, 8 and 9; Opus reviews every Fable PR before the owner merges.

### Implementation clarifications, Package 1 (for review with its PR)

16. **The shell.** The header is the wordmark (to Today), the Today/Forward switch and a ⌂ menu on the right that replaces M1's settings glyph; Settings is reached through the menu. The menu is a `<details>` disclosure, so it works without JavaScript; with JavaScript it closes on navigation, Escape and a tap outside. `src/ui/places.ts` lists the quiet places in the agreed order with a `ready` flag, and the menu shows only places whose page exists (a unit test ties the flag to the page file), so in Package 1 it holds only Settings. Family, Us and Life admin are removed from the list until they exist. A skip link leads every page. The capture bar has a slot at the foot of the shell that renders nothing until Package 7.
17. **The gate sits in the domain write path.** `auditedWrite` checks it before opening its transaction, so a refused write locks and writes nothing; the context no-op update and `approveMany` (which would otherwise report each proposal as refused) check it themselves. The audit row for an explicit sensitive read is written by a separate, ungated `auditRead`: auditing what a person read stays operational while the gate is closed. `realDataGateOpen` reads the raw environment on every call; `HOME_REAL_DATA` is accepted by the environment schema as any string, so no value can stop boot. Proven across every exported domain write function by the privacy suite's completeness list, for seven closed values.
18. **Accessibility baseline.** `--muted` is the accessible Mist and `--muted-mark` the original (§5); text never uses `--muted-mark` (unit-tested). The focus ring is Pine (Moon at night), not Sun: Sun on Morning is about 1.9:1, below the 3:1 a focus indicator needs, and Sun means "needs you". Motion is cut for `prefers-reduced-motion`. Sun-coloured text on the sign-out and error "Try again" buttons (about 1.9:1) is replaced by the quiet button. The sign-in email field no longer suppresses its focus outline.
19. **Form pattern.** Server actions use `formAction` (`src/app/_forms/action.ts`): the actor from the session, the domain service validating with its own Zod schema, and every error turned into calm copy (`src/app/_forms/error-copy.ts`, typed as a complete record of domain refusal codes, so a new code cannot ship without copy). Field errors are keyed by field name and never echo input; unexpected errors log only the error's class name.
20. **E2E data.** The e2e global setup seeds the synthetic fixture family into the local test database with the existing launcher and guard (ADR 0005 §41; local and CI only). Screen specs sign in as a fixture adult through the real magic-link flow, and run an axe scan (`@axe-core/playwright`, a dev dependency) for WCAG 2.x A/AA with no serious or critical violations, plus a no-horizontal-scroll check, at 375×812, 768×1024, 1024×768 and 1280×800.

### Implementation clarifications, Package 2 (for review with its PR)

21. **Export contents.** The export is composed only of existing reads, called with the actor: people, events and their annotations, projects, tasks, notes, context (every status; archived included), captures, proposals, conversations and messages, and Activity (`listAudit`, paged to the end). Two small own-record reads were added for the two owner-only tables that had none: `listOwnResponses` (insight responses) and `listOwnUsage` (the actor's own Kev runs; each run is private to its user, §43 of ADR 0005, so they are the actor's to take). Archived records are included and marked by `archivedAt`. Ids are preserved; each type is ordered by its sort column (`createdAt`, or `respondedAt`/`at` where that is the table's time), then id. Kev and the system are refused (`not_a_user`, `not_eligible`): an export is a person's own download, from a screen.
22. **Format version 1** is `src/domain/export/spec.ts`: `format`, `version`, `exportedAt`, `timeZone`, `exportedBy.personId`, **`includesSensitive`** (added, so a file says whether it holds sensitive items), and `records` with one array per type. Each record carries exactly its listed columns (instants as ISO strings, bigints as strings); the Zod schema `exportV1` is strict, so a file holds nothing it does not declare. Excluded on purpose, with reasons in the spec: Better Auth's tables and `rate_limit` (authentication secrets and counters), and `audit_log.visibility`/`visible_to_user_id` (the P-1 snapshot that decides who sees a row; Activity never shows it). `tests/unit/export-completeness.test.ts` fails on any table or column that is neither exported nor excluded.
23. **Sensitive items.** Only the unticked **Include sensitive items** box asks for them; the export service passes the box's value to the context service's existing audited read, so each sensitive record returned writes its own `context.sensitive_read` row. The static guard now fails if `includeSensitive` is named anywhere outside the context service, the trust predicate and the three reviewed export call sites (`tests/unit/sensitive-context-guard.test.ts`); the previous pattern match missed the shorthand `{ includeSensitive }`.
24. **Audit.** Each download writes `export.download`, private to the adult (P-1 snapshot), with counts per record type and the sensitive flag as its only meta. Export audit rows (and sensitive-read rows) are written through the ungated `auditRead`: taking your data away is a read and works while the real-data gate is closed.
25. **The route.** The page is `/settings/export`; the download is `POST /settings/export/download`, because Next.js cannot serve a page and a route handler from one path. It takes the actor from the session (303 to sign-in without one), refuses a cross-site request (403 when `Origin` names another host, or `Sec-Fetch-Site: cross-site`), and answers with `Cache-Control: no-store` and `Content-Disposition: attachment`. The page is a plain form, so it works without JavaScript.
26. **Export check.** `node scripts/check-export.mts <file>` validates a downloaded file against version 1 and prints only counts, for DEPLOY.md §E item 9.

27. **Accepted by the owner, 2026-10-04, at the PR #32 review:**
    - **Download route.** The export download stays at `POST /settings/export/download` (§25).
    - **Kev usage.** Each adult's export includes their own Kev usage runs (§21).
    - **`includesSensitive`.** The top-level field stays in version 1 of the format (§22).
    - **Restore rehearsal timing.** The full `home-dev` restore rehearsal (DEPLOY.md §E item 7) is deferred until M3 has at least one domain-entry screen, so its before-and-after markers are synthetic household records rather than sign-ins. It remains mandatory before `HOME_REAL_DATA=open`.

### Implementation clarifications, Package 3 (for review with its PR)

28. **"Add me" is one domain transaction.** `createAndLinkSelf(actor, { name })` in the People service creates a household parent and links the acting user to it inside one transaction, so a refused link (already linked, or a lost race) leaves no stray person. It is otherwise `createPerson` then `linkSelf`, unchanged; it refuses Kev and the system, and the privacy suite's completeness list covers it.
29. **Linked-person rules on screen.** A linked person's edit form shows role ("Parent") and visibility ("Everyone at home") as fixed text with the reason, instead of controls that would be refused on submit, and the normal form does not submit those fields. A crafted post that does send them is still refused by the People service (`linked_person`), which is where the rule lives. The profile of a linked person offers no Archive; it says whose record it is and points to Settings › You. Everything else is editable as usual; a refusal the service makes anyway (`referenced_by_household`, `not_creator`) is read under the form in the calm copy.
30. **Profile contents in Package 3.** *Things to know* is the default context read about the person (normal context only; sensitive never appears here). *Coming up* shows the next birthday from the profile engine and nothing else until Package 5 brings events through the agenda engine. Notes about the person are listed read-only; adding and editing notes on their subject arrives with Package 6 (contract §3.7). Age is shown in whole years beside the role, as the profile engine computes it for the home time zone's today.
31. **Settings › You.** When the adult is linked, the page names their person with **Not me** (an inline confirmation) and explains the linked-person rules. When not, it lists unlinked household parents with **This is me** and an **Add me** form. Linking another adult is impossible by construction: the list never offers a linked person, and the services act only on the signed-in user's own link. Today shows "Which one is you? ›" only while the adult has no linked person.
32. **Forms.** `ActionForm`, `ConfirmAction` and the person form are client components over `useActionState`, with the calm message under the controls; they still submit and show their outcome without JavaScript (tested). Checkboxes travel with a presence marker (`<name>_present`), so an unticked box reads as false and a form that never showed the box leaves the field alone.

33. **Review fixes (Opus review of PR #33).** A refused save (validation, a domain rule or the closed gate) hands the submitted text fields back in the form state, to the same browser only and never logged, and the person form and the Add-me form re-render with them, so nothing typed is lost, with or without JavaScript. The edit reader sends only fields the form carried: an absent colour is not cleared and an absent "lives at home" box is not ticked. `updatePerson` writes and audits only the fields whose values change; a patch that changes nothing is a no-op with no audit row (still gated). While the Production real-data gate is closed, Today shows no "Which one is you?" prompt and Settings › You says family data entry isn't open yet instead of offering link, add or unlink. Each "This is me" button's accessible name includes the person's name. The profile reads "age 9", and context categories are shown in plain words.

34. **Focus after a refused save.** Every M3 form that shows FormState uses `useRefusalFocus` (`src/app/_forms/focus.ts`): after a refusal, keyboard focus moves to the first field marked invalid or, when the refusal names no field, to the form's message (focusable, `data-form-message`). Without JavaScript the browser reloads the page as usual. This closes the carried-forward Package 3 review item.

### Implementation clarifications, Package 4 (for review with its PR)

35. **Recurrence model and storage.** `src/domain/engines/recurrence.ts` builds the approved presets as RFC 5545 rules: `FREQ=DAILY`; `FREQ=WEEKLY;BYDAY=…` (days always named); fortnightly as `FREQ=WEEKLY;INTERVAL=2;BYDAY=…`; `FREQ=MONTHLY`; `FREQ=YEARLY`; ending never, `COUNT=n` (1–1000) or `UNTIL`. For an all-day event `UNTIL` is the date (`UNTIL=20261231`); for a timed one it is the last second of that local day in the event's zone, in UTC, as RFC 5545 requires. `readRRule` reads a stored rule back into the model; any other rule (another interval, ordinal weekdays, `BYMONTHDAY`, `BYSETPOS`, `WKST`, `COUNT` with `UNTIL`, …) is `custom` and kept exactly as written.
36. **Expansion and DST.** Rules are expanded in floating local time with `rrule`, never with its own time-zone support; each occurrence's local wall clock is then turned into an instant in the event's IANA zone by `instantFromWallClock` (`src/lib/dates.ts`): ordinary times keep their wall clock across both transitions, a time in the spring-forward gap moves forward by the gap (02:30 → 03:30 NZDT), and a time in the repeated autumn hour takes its first instance (NZDT). A timed occurrence keeps the event's elapsed length; an all-day occurrence keeps its number of days and never touches a zone. A custom rule is still expanded the same way; a rule that cannot be parsed yields only the event's first occurrence, never a guess. `COUNT` counts from the first occurrence before exdates are removed (RFC 5545).
37. **Skipping an occurrence.** An exdate that is a date (`YYYY-MM-DD`, or RFC's `YYYYMMDD`) skips the occurrence starting that day (the event's local day for timed events); an exdate that is an instant skips the timed occurrence starting at exactly that moment; anything else is ignored. `skipOccurrence` adds a date exdate, which is what Package 5's "skip this one" writes.
38. **The agenda engine.** `src/domain/engines/agenda.ts` takes records the caller has already read through the domain services (so visibility, archive and sensitivity are applied) and a range of dates in the home zone (at most 400 days), and returns the days that have anything, in order. Within a day, all-day items come first, in this order: all-day events, birthdays, project target dates, tasks due; then timed events by start. Ties break by title, then id, so the result never depends on input order. An all-day event appears on every day it covers (with its day of the span); a timed event on the day it starts in the home zone, which may differ from the event's own zone. Only open tasks and projects not done are included. Birthdays use the profile engine's rule (P-4), with the age reached. `forPerson` keeps a person's events (attending or responsible) and birthday, for Coming up. No screen uses it yet: Today, Forward and Coming up consume it in Packages 5 and 9.
39. **Dependency.** `rrule` (approved stack, CLAUDE.md) is the one new runtime dependency, used only inside the recurrence engine. No migration: `event.rrule` and `event.exdates` already hold everything (ADR 0006 §9).

### Implementation clarifications, Package 5 (for review with its PR)

40. **Event writes in one transaction.** `setEventPeople(actor, eventId, people)` makes an event's annotations exactly a set (the event locked first, then missing ones set and extra ones removed, each through the existing `setEventPerson` / `removeEventPerson`, so every rule and audit row is as before); `createEventWithPeople` creates an event and annotates it; `editEventWithPeople(actor, id, patch, people)` edits an event and its people as one change, in this order: lock the event, remove the people no longer wanted, update the fields (visibility among them), set the newly wanted people, every step audited in the same transaction. So a private event with a private person on it can become household-visible in one save when that person comes off in the same edit, and a refused person (`references_private`, not found) rolls the whole edit back: the fields and the annotations stay as they were. All three refuse Kev and the system and are in the privacy suite's completeness list. The edit form uses `editEventWithPeople`; nothing in the UI saves an event in two writes. (Opus review of PR #35, finding I1: the earlier two-write edit could half-save, and could not make that visibility change in one save.)
41. **The event form.** Times are entered as a date, a from and a to time (and, only when something runs past midnight, an end date) in the home time zone; an existing event keeps its own zone, which the form never shows. An all-day event's last day is shown inclusive and stored exclusive (ADR 0005 §26). The people a form may name are the people the actor can see, read in the action rather than trusted from the form, in two groups: going and responsible. The repeat controls are the approved model only; a stored custom rule is shown as "Repeats (custom)", kept as it is, and saving leaves it alone. Choosing "Doesn't repeat" also clears the skipped dates. Refusals the reader makes before the domain sees the input (a bad time, no weekday, an end before the first time) are field errors through `FormFieldError`.
42. **An event's page.** When, how it repeats and its kind on one line; where and notes; who is going and who is responsible (links to their profiles); for a repeating event, the next few times in the next eight weeks, each with **Skip this one** (an exdate by date, ADR 0006 §37), and the upcoming skipped dates with **Put back** (the exdate removed again), which is the inverse of the same rule and not a single-occurrence edit. Both are domain writes that judge the date themselves (`skipEventOccurrence`, `putBackEventOccurrence`): a skip is refused unless the event repeats (`not_recurring`) and its rule puts an occurrence on that date by the recurrence engine (`not_an_occurrence`), so a one-off event can never be hidden by a crafted post; a put back is refused unless the date is in the skipped dates (`not_skipped`). Each refusal has calm copy. The skipped list shows only dates from today on that the current rule still reaches; a past skip, or one a changed rule no longer reaches, is not something to put back. A synced event says "This comes from a calendar, so change it there" and offers no edit, skip or archive; its edit URL redirects to its page. Archive and Restore as every record; archiving returns to Forward. (Opus review of PR #35, finding I2.)
43. **Forward and Coming up share one loader.** `src/app/_agenda/load.ts` reads events (with their people), people, open tasks and projects through the domain services as the signed-in adult and hands them to the agenda engine; `agenda-list.tsx` renders the days the same way everywhere (a label per day, "Today ·" and "Tomorrow ·" prefixes, "All day" or the start time, people as dots, from the loader's full set of people on every screen, so Coming up shows the same dots as Forward). Screens do no date work of their own: an event's engine view comes from `src/domain/events/occurrences.ts`, wall-clock times from `lib/dates`. Forward is today plus 29 days; a person's Coming up is `forPerson` over the same 30 days. Tasks and project targets appear without links until Package 6 builds their pages. Today will use the same loader in Package 9.

### Implementation clarifications, Package 6 (for review with its PR)

44. **Home.** `/home` groups projects by status under *Under way*, *Ideas* and *Paused*, in title order within each; *Done* is folded away with its count. A row is the title, its line and the date it aims for. The form asks what, a line about it, where it's at and a date to aim for; visibility is under More. Domain is always `home` (V0.1). A project's page shows its line, its tasks through the shared task list (open first, done and dropped folded) with "Add a task" that opens the task form already part of it, and its notes. Archive returns to Home; an archived project is shown with Restore and its notes read-only.
45. **To do.** `/tasks` lists open tasks by due date (none last), then project title, then age, the same order on a project's page (`orderTasks`, unit-tested); done and dropped are folded away with their count. A row is the due date, the title (a link), its project and who is doing it. An overdue open task carries the one Sun mark, with "Overdue" for assistive technology; nothing is red. **Done** sits on every open row: one tap, through `setTaskStatusAction`, which returns to where it was pressed with `?undo=<id>` so the page offers *Undo* (back to open) in place; **Drop it** and **Back to To do** live on the task's page the same way. Status is never read from the edit form. A task has no separate edit route: its page carries the form (title, then under More: notes, part of, who's doing it, who it's about, due, how long in minutes, needs, a window to do it, visibility). The project and people the form may name for a new link are the ones the actor can see, read in the action rather than trusted from the form. An edit sends a reference only when it changed, so a project or person the task already holds (shown as "… (archived)" once archived) is kept when left alone rather than cleared or refused. Done, Drop it and Undo return to the page they were pressed on (To do or the project), the address bound by that page and accepted only as a parsed same-origin path (`localPath`), with Undo offered there and focused on arrival. An unknown status reads as `not_eligible`. A scheduled window is a date, a from and a to time in the home zone, both ends or none; an inverted or half window is a field error before the domain sees it.
46. **Notes on their subject.** One block (`src/app/_notes/notes-section.tsx`) renders a subject's notes on a project, person or event page: newest first, each with the date and "just me" when private, *Change* (the words, and who can see it) and *Archive this note* in place; *Add a note* at the foot; archived notes folded away with *Restore*, so a note is restored where it lives rather than only from Settings › Archived (Package 8 lists them too). The subject and the return address are bound by the server-rendered page into the action, so they travel through the form like any field and are readable in the browser; they are never trusted: the service re-checks that the subject is visible and not archived and that a household note never points at something private, and the return address is accepted only as a parsed same-origin path. A note on a private subject is private, and the choice is not offered. An archived subject shows its notes read-only. Because note records now live on an event's page, the event's own free text is labelled *Details* (form and page), so "Notes" means one thing.
47. **Links on Forward.** Task due dates and project target dates on Forward and Coming up link to their pages now that those exist; `places.ts` marks Home and To do ready, so the ⌂ menu lists People, Home, To do and Settings.
48. **Package 5 minors carried.** The audit-order assertion in the event edit test compares the three rows as a set (one transaction, one clock); the event form defaults use `clockOf` and `isoDateInZone` rather than their own formatting.

### Implementation clarifications, Package 7 (for review with its PR)

49. **`organiseCapture`.** `organiseCapture(actor, captureId, { action, payload, summary })` in `src/domain/proposals/service.ts` is `createProposal` then `approveProposal`, in one transaction, as the person: the payload is validated by the target's own schema before anything is stored (a refusal stores nothing and the form shows the fields); an execution the target service refuses returns the stored `failed` proposal with its fixed code, and the executor's savepoint leaves nothing of what it tried. It accepts only the five organising actions (`task.create`, `event.create`, `project.create`, `note.create`, `context.create`; anything else is `not_eligible`), refuses Kev and the system, and is in the privacy suite's completeness list (so the closed real-data gate refuses it too). The record gets `origin_capture_id` and `created_via = 'ui'` from the execution token; context so created is sourced `capture` and is never sensitive (a proposal cannot write sensitive context); the capture's `organised_into` and status are settled by the existing `settleCapture`. Calling it again on an organised capture makes another record (Make another). There is no other organising path.
50. **The capture bar.** In the shell's slot on every place: one text box, "Tell HOME something…", and **Keep**. The words are sent and stored exactly as typed (no trim, no parsing); "✓ Kept · in To sort" appears only when the action returns success, and then the box empties. A refusal (only spaces, the closed gate, anything unexpected) keeps the words in the box with a calm line ending "Your words are still here." and returns focus to it. It is a plain server-action form, so it works without JavaScript. The page scrolls with `scroll-padding` for the sticky header and bar, so a focused field is never hidden under either (found when the bar first covered a form's submit button on a phone).
51. **To sort.** `/sort` lists the actor's own `new` and `proposed` captures, newest first, the words exactly (line breaks kept) with a soft time from `softWhen` (just now, 12 min ago, today 09:40, yesterday, the weekday, then the date; never an age, never a colour). Each has **Make it a…** and **Not needed**. A capture's own page, `/sort/[id]`, shows its words, what it became (links to each record, read back as the actor so each record's own visibility applies) and the five choices (*Make another* once it is something); `/sort/[id]/[as]` (`task`, `event`, `project`, `note`, `know`) is the normal form for that record, prefilled from the words and all editable. These two sub-routes of `/sort` are where "opens the normal form" lives; the contract's route table lists `/sort` only. Only the capture's author reaches them; anyone else reads "Nothing here."
52. **The prefilled forms.** Task, event and project use their own forms with the words as the title (a capture longer than a title allows is refused as too long, to be shortened by the person; nothing is cut automatically). The event form leaves out who's going: an annotation is a second proposal action, and one organise is one proposal; people are added on the event's page afterwards, which the form says. A note chooses its subject (a person, a project or an event the actor can see) with the words as its body. *Something to know* chooses who or what it is about (everyone at home, a person or a project), the words as its content, and a kind; never sensitive (that is made directly, under What Kev knows). The subjects offered are read on the server for the form and again in the action, so an id the form did not offer is refused.
53. **Not needed, Undo, Bring it back.** Not needed is `dismissCapture`: the capture leaves To sort, "Set aside. Nothing is deleted." appears in place with Undo focused (`undismissCapture`, back to what its proposals and organisation make it). A set-aside capture's page offers *Bring it back*. Dismissed captures are listed under Archived in Package 8; copy never says deleted (§8).
54. **Today's line.** Today (still the Package 9 placeholder) shows "Two things to sort ›", in words, linking to To sort, only when the actor has `new` or `proposed` captures. No Sun mark, no count badge. `places.ts` marks To sort ready, so the ⌂ menu lists People, Home, To do, To sort and Settings.

### Implementation clarifications, Package 8 (for review with its PR)

55. **What Kev knows.** `/settings/knows` lists the context the signed-in adult can see (the default read: normal sensitivity only), grouped by subject in the order everyone at home, each person, each project (a subject the adult cannot see now is left out with its rows). Within a group, items the staleness engine judges possibly out of date come first, each with a gentle line ("Still true? Not confirmed since 1 Mar." or "…It was meant to hold until 2 Mar."), then newest first; retired items are folded away under "No longer true". Each item offers **Still true** (`confirmContext`), **No longer true** (`retireContext`) or **Reinstate**, **Change** (the words, kind, holds-until, who can see it) and **Archive**, in place. Kinds read in words (`CATEGORY_LABEL`); no enum name is shown. **Show sensitive items** is the one screen where a person asks for sensitive context: a press posts a form whose own field sets `includeSensitive`, the service's audited sensitive read runs (`context.sensitive_read` per item), and the items come back in that response alone, listed under their own heading with the same actions; nothing is stored, nothing is in the URL, and leaving the page hides them again. Kev and the system cannot press anything, and the service refuses them the option regardless. `tests/unit/sensitive-context-guard.test.ts` names this call site deliberately. **Add something to know** at the foot creates context directly: who or what it is about, the words, a kind, and under More a holds-until date, who can see it and a sensitivity choice explained in plain words; sensitivity is decided once, when it is made (Change never offers it).
56. **Archived.** `/settings/archived` reads every record type through its own service with `includeArchived` as the adult and keeps the archived ones: people, events, projects, tasks, notes, things to know (active and retired; sensitive stays out, this being a default read), captures, and conversations with Kev; plus the adult's captures set aside from To sort (`status: 'dismissed'`), which offer **Back to To sort** (`undismissCapture`) rather than Restore. Grouped by type, newest first, each with Restore through the record's own restore service (which checks it is the adult's to see and in fact archived). The copy says archived, set aside, put away and restore; never deleted (ADR 0006 §8).
57. **Activity.** `/settings/activity` reads 50 rows a page with the audit cursor (`?before=<id>`, the id of the last row shown), so "Earlier ›" is a link that works without JavaScript and never skips or repeats a row; "‹ Latest" returns. Events read in plain words (`describeEvent`: exact sentences for the known events, else the type and action in words), who did it reads as "by hand", "through Kev", "from a calendar" or "HOME". Rows show what happened and the row's own summary, never written content; the M2 rules are unchanged: rows about sensitive items are never listed, and a row about a private record only to the adult who can see it.
58. **Settings and carried minors.** Settings lists You · What Kev knows · Archived · Activity · Export (no Calendars, no Usage). The capture bar is keyed by the path, so "✓ Kept · in To sort" is said on the place where it was said and not carried to the next page; a capture's line breaks become spaces when its words prefill a one-line title (a note or something to know keeps them).
