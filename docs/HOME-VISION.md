# HOME — Vision

> HOME is the private operating layer for our family. Kev is the intelligence that powers it.

## The problem

A family runs on a lot of moving parts: two work calendars, children's activities, school terms, appointments, birthdays, a house that always needs something, cars that need servicing, subscriptions that renew, and a relationship that deserves time of its own.

The information exists, but it is scattered across calendars, message threads, notes apps, email and people's heads. The work of *holding it all together* — noticing the clash on Thursday, remembering the WOF is due, finding the Saturday morning to finally paint the fence — falls on whoever happens to be paying attention. That is tiring, and things slip.

Existing tools don't solve this. Family calendars show *what is scheduled* but not *what it means*. Task managers turn family life into administration. Dashboards give you more to look at, not less to think about.

## The idea

HOME is not a calendar, a task manager or a dashboard. It is a calm place that understands our family's context and helps us:

1. **See what is happening** — today, clearly.
2. **See what matters next** — the coming week, month and season, with issues surfaced before they are urgent.
3. **Get help** — ask Kev, in plain language, and get a useful, grounded answer or a sensible proposal.

The primary interface is conversation — typed today, spoken eventually. We should rarely need to decide where information belongs. We tell Kev; HOME keeps structured information underneath.

**Capture first, organise second.** Anything we tell HOME is kept immediately, in our own words, even if nobody has decided yet whether it is a task, an event, a project, a note or something to remember about someone. Kev then suggests how it should be organised. Nothing is lost because it didn't fit a box.

**People are at the centre.** HOME understands the people behind the calendar — who they are, their age and stage, their regular week, what they enjoy — not just the appointments attached to their names.

> "Kev, what does our weekend look like?"
> "Add fixing the garage light to the house."
> "When could I realistically get that done?"
> "Find us a night where Courtney and I could go out."

## Who it is for

One family. Ours. HOME is a private system for a single household, not a product for the market. That constraint is a feature: it lets us optimise for trust, taste and usefulness rather than scale, onboarding funnels or monetisation.

Initial users are the two parents. Children appear in HOME as people the family cares about — with activities, birthdays and interests — not as users or subjects of tracking.

## What it should feel like

Walking into an organised, calm home. Warm, spacious, legible, quiet. The intelligence is powerful underneath and nearly invisible on the surface. Nothing about it should look like enterprise software, Jira, a CRM or a wall of widgets.

## The six domains (long-term)

| Domain | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Today** | The family picture for today: who is where, what needs doing, what to know. |
| **Forward** | 7 / 30 / 90-day horizon: events, deadlines, decisions and conflicts before they bite. |
| **Family** | Activities, experiences, traditions, milestones and memories. Human, positive — never clinical. |
| **Us** | A private space for the parents: date ideas, time together, things one of us has mentioned. Never surveillance or scoring. |
| **Home** | Projects around the physical house: ideas, photos, measurements, materials, costs, progress, decisions. |
| **Life Admin** | Vehicles, insurance, subscriptions, warranties, maintenance, renewals, documents. Low friction. |

Domains are a way of *presenting* the family's information. They are not silos. Kev reasons across them.

## Kev

Kev is not a chatbot bolted onto an app. Kev is the reasoning layer across the whole family system. HOME's permanent trust model — for V0.1 and for every future capability — is:

```
OBSERVE → UNDERSTAND → RECOMMEND → HUMAN APPROVAL → ACT
```

Kev never makes consequential decisions on its own. Kev earns autonomy gradually, one well-understood capability at a time.

Kev's rule of thumb: **code computes, Kev explains.** Dates, permissions, conflicts, weather windows and free time are calculated deterministically; Kev reasons over those results and communicates them. Kev never invents them.

The signature behaviour is **cross-domain reasoning**. A home project needs three dry hours; Kev considers the calendar, work, the kids' activities, the forecast and existing priorities, and says: *"Saturday 9:00–12:00 looks like the cleanest window."*

## The Week Ahead

A recurring family briefing, written by Kev, focused on **anticipation rather than administration**:

- Needs coordination
- Potential conflicts
- Family opportunities
- Home
- You two
- Coming over the horizon

## What success looks like

The first question HOME must answer is:

> **Does having Kev understand our family context make everyday family coordination meaningfully easier?**

If yes, we grow HOME domain by domain. If no, we learn why before building more.

Long-term, success is: fewer things slip, fewer last-minute scrambles, projects that actually get finished, and more intentional time together — with *less* effort spent managing it all.
